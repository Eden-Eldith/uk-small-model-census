\section{Capability that existing inventories may not see}
British AI sovereignty depends on identifying the people who can already
design, train and develop models in the United Kingdom. An independent
researcher who builds a language model from randomly initialised parameters
has created domestic technical capability, whether the work was performed
in an academic laboratory, a funded company or a home workshop. If national
mapping begins with registered businesses and university networks, such
individuals need not appear in the initial population at all.

The government's \emph{Artificial Intelligence Sector Study 2025}, published
on 29 October 2026, measures commercial AI activity through UK-registered
companies. Its methods already use frontier language models and agentic
research tools for discovering and classifying firms. Its stated population
excludes non-registered individual contractors \cite{sectorstudy}.
\textbf{The absence of an independent developer from a company census is
a consequence of its population definition, not evidence that the developer
does not exist.} That boundary matters when an economic inventory becomes
the starting point for understanding national technical capability.

The Council for Science and Technology's advice, published on 19 October
2026, calls for a Sovereignty Plan informed by a comprehensive view of
the domestic AI ecosystem \cite{cst}. Will that map search Hugging Face,
GitHub, Zenodo and individual builders, or reproduce the institutions it
already knows? The practical question is who has already built domestic
models outside university and institutional routes, and how to find them.

The author trained WiggleGPT, approximately 124 million parameters, from
scratch in Gosport on personally owned consumer GPUs \cite{wigglegpt,wigglecard}.
Six earlier manual and AI-assisted investigations did not identify another
matching independent UK builder. The author does not believe he is the
only one: this search is built to find the others. The purpose is to discover potential collaborators
and people whose demonstrated skills warrant research backing.

\section{Discoverable models, undiscovered builders}
WiggleGPT is publicly available with weights, code and an archived paper.
The \emph{State of AI Report} presents itself as a widely read and trusted
independent annual overview of AI progress \cite{stateofaiabout}.
Its 2026 edition, dated 8 October and covering the preceding
12 months, contains no mention of WiggleGPT or its author in its searchable
text \cite{stateofai2026}. The earliest saved Zenodo record gives an original
publication date of 25 November 2025 and a deposit created on 8 December
2025 \cite{wiggleoriginal}. The work therefore predates the report and falls
within its stated reporting window. The omission concerns the already
released original work.

The author's search experience makes the mechanism tangible. Generic
Gosport AI searches often surfaced institutional pages or jobs instead of
his work. Yahoo surfaced his Grokipedia entry where Google did not; he reads
this as relevant results being downranked. In his earlier agent-assisted
searches, broad questions about independent British model builders produced
answers that they did not exist, supported by familiar official archives.
Agents given this Hugging Face search found WiggleGPT immediately.
Asking universities who does AI also
privileges the people already connected to those universities.

The implication is direct: a capability map that consults only company
registers and established institutions cannot establish the absence of
independent capability. It has not searched the population in which that
capability may reside. Released checkpoints, training code and archival
research provide a practical alternative entry point. They can reveal
skills before the builder gains an employer, grant or incorporated company.

\section{From inventory to opportunity}
The constructive purpose is to make talent visible. A public index of
independent builders would let research groups, compute providers, companies
and public bodies find documented work, identify collaborators and determine
where support could accelerate it. Technical capability should be
discoverable before a researcher learns to market a company or builds a
large social-media audience.

Educational access helps explain why university networks are an incomplete
starting point. The Department for Education's July 2026 release follows
English state-school pupils aged 15 in 2020/21 to age 19 in 2024/25. It reports
higher-education progression of 44.7\% overall and 28.6\% among
pupils eligible for free school meals; the corresponding rate for White
British boys eligible for free school meals is 13.0\% \cite{dfe2026}.
The June 2026 \emph{Independent Inquiry into White Working Class Educational
Outcomes} also identifies working-class disadvantage beyond FSM eligibility
\cite{wwci}. University networks therefore leave a substantial route into
technical learning and capability outside their normal field of view.

Learning to train a small language model can start much earlier than a
research degree. Students can initialise weights, observe loss decline,
generate imperfect text, change the architecture and see the result. A
character-level Shakespeare model gives this process a concrete form.
Schools, after-school clubs, community programmes and apprenticeships could
teach Python, PyTorch, training, evaluation and deployment. Kaggle provides
free, quota-limited GPU notebooks \cite{kagglegpu}; Google's September 2026 announcement
extends premium Colab benefits to Google AI subscribers \cite{colab2026}.
Publicly explaining these routes would help people use access they may
already have.

The author's route combined practical hardware experience, growing up
helping in his father's computer shop, with self-directed learning; he dates
his start in Python to 17 January 2025. His work was assisted by AI. He
directed Astra to carry out WiggleGPT's task-adaptation work with the
instruction to make it do what GPT-1 did. The resulting task evaluations are
documented in the model release \cite{wigglecard}. AI assistance can put
capability in places established discovery systems have not thought to look.

Regional compute access, machine-learning apprenticeships and technical hubs
would connect those beginnings to sustained research. Gosport illustrates
why the search should extend beyond the familiar university and technology
centres. Wider infrastructure planning must account for electricity
distribution, cooling and long-term resilience. Funding only established
performers misses the next capable researcher. Regional access and practical
learning routes create a path for that researcher to emerge and be recognised.

Publication access is another part of discoverability. arXiv requires
endorsement for a first submission to a new endorsement category, with
automatic endorsement possible in specified circumstances; institutional
email can assist the process \cite{arxivendorsement}. Zenodo and model
repositories offer additional routes for independent work to become public.
A national inventory should follow reproducible outputs across these venues
instead of treating participation in one academic publication route as the
boundary of research capability.

\section{Evidence coverage}
The census covers the specified 917-model search on 30 October 2026.
Public evidence leaves 151 account locations and 385 model origins
unresolved. Six of the seven UK development entries use dated professional
or study-location inferences; WiggleGPT has direct project-location evidence.
Lesci's joint-first-author role establishes participation in PolyPythias;
the exact division of training operations is unrecorded. Project sponsorship
and some hardware details remain unknown. The appendix retains the sources,
evidence levels and open fields for every account.

\section{What about the independents?}

Britain's ability to develop sovereign AI depends on whether it knows who
can already build the technology. The independent case found here is public
and inspectable. The people paid to discover sovereign capability should
have found publicly released independent work on the main model-hosting
platform. That failure is \emph{a major indictment of their ability to
capture sovereign AI talent and capability}. The criticism is constructive: the aim is for the
people responsible for discovery to do better, and for the builders they
find to have a route to collaboration and sustained support.

\textbf{If Britain wants to know what sovereign AI capability it already
has, it must look not only where companies and universities report it, but
where independent individuals actually build and publish it.} Becoming a
producer rather than merely a consumer of AI includes identifying, teaching,
supporting and retaining those individuals.
